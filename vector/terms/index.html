The deal, in plain English.

These are the terms for using Vector, the roulette tracking app for iPhone. I have written them the way I would say them out loud, because terms nobody reads protect nobody. Using the app means you accept what is on this page.

Last updated 1 September 2026.

What Vector is

Vector is a tracking and analysis tool. You type in the numbers a roulette wheel produces, and it keeps the record — the spins, the distances between them, and what has happened at the table so far.

From that record it works out where the ball is likely to land next, and it shows you that before the spin resolves — a sector of the wheel, and how far it sits from where the ball started. If you want to know what twenty pockets away from the release point looks like, Vector will tell you, in time for it to be useful.

Plenty of people run it against free-to-play tables, where Gold Coins cost nothing and risk nothing, purely to see how well the call holds up. That is a perfectly good way to use it, and the one I would point a new user at first.

What Vector is not: it does not accept wagers, does not handle money, and is not a gambling service. It has no connection to any casino and no involvement in anything you do at one.

You have to be 18 or over

Vector is for adults only. By using it you confirm you are at least 18 years old, and that gambling is legal where you are. Knowing the law where you live is your responsibility, not mine — it varies enormously and I cannot advise you on it.

What the call is, and what it is not

Vector gives you a real number to work with. What it does not give you is a promise, and the difference matters enough to put it up here rather than in the small print.

Roulette outcomes are random

Every spin is independent of the one before it. The wheel has no memory. Nothing in the history of a table changes the odds of the next number, and no amount of recorded data changes that fact.

A call is not a guarantee

Vector tells you where it expects the ball to land. It cannot tell you where the ball will land, and it does not promise you will win anything. Treat the call as information to decide with, not as an outcome you have been sold.

I have not proven an edge

I have backtested the method against recorded spins and it has not beaten chance in the data I have so far. I would rather say that plainly than imply otherwise and have you find out yourself. The accuracy record is built into the app so you can judge it against your own tables instead of taking my word for any of it.

So what are you paying for?

The call, made fast enough to act on, and the record that lets you check it. On free-to-play tables that costs you nothing to test. What you decide to do with it after that is yours.

Vector comes with no warranty of any kind. I have built it carefully and I use it myself, but I cannot promise it will be free of bugs, always available, or fit for any particular purpose. Software written by one person sometimes gets things wrong.

Buying, subscriptions and renewals

Recording spins, the distances and the accuracy record stay free. The prediction feature is what is sold, and every purchase goes through Apple. Apple takes the payment and handles the billing — I never see your card or payment details.

Monthly subscription

Auto-renewing. It renews each month unless you cancel at least 24 hours before the current period ends.

Annual subscription

Auto-renewing. It renews each year unless you cancel at least 24 hours before the current period ends.

Lifetime unlock

A one-time purchase, not a subscription. Nothing renews and there is nothing to cancel. You can restore it on your other devices with the same Apple Account.

Subscriptions are managed in your Apple Account settings, not inside Vector — that is where you go to cancel or to change plan, and cancelling takes effect at the end of the period you have already paid for. Refunds are handled by Apple under their terms, because Apple is who you paid.

If it stops being entertainment

Gambling can be addictive. If you are chasing losses, betting money you need, hiding it from people close to you, or you have stopped enjoying it and cannot stop anyway, that is worth taking seriously.

Help is free, confidential, and staffed by people who have heard it all before and will not lecture you.

In the United States, the National Council on Problem Gambling runs a free twenty-four-hour helpline — ncpgambling.org. In the United Kingdom and much of the rest of the world, BeGambleAware.org offers the same. Both publish their current numbers on their own sites, which is why this page sends you there rather than printing a number that could quietly go out of date.

Please use one. No feature in this app matters more than that.
