Vector · Privacy

Vector collects nothing and sends nothing.

That is the entire policy. The rest of this page just explains what that means and why I can say it so flatly, because a privacy policy that takes ten minutes to read is usually hiding something.

Last updated 1 September 2026.

There is nowhere for your data to go

Vector has no server. It contains no networking code of any kind — not for analytics, not for advertising, not for crash reports, not for anything. There are no third-party SDKs in it. There is no account to create and no login, because there is nothing to log in to.

This is not a promise about how carefully I handle your data. It is simpler than that: the app has no way to send anything anywhere, so nothing about you reaches me. In Apple's App Privacy questions this is the answer marked Data Not Collected, and it is the truthful one.

What is stored, and where

Everything Vector records stays in the app's own storage on your iPhone. Specifically:

Your current table

A file called current-session.json holding the table you are sitting at right now — the spin numbers, when they happened, and the settings for that table.

Your finished tables

A file called sessions.json holding the tables you have already closed out, so the accuracy record can be built from them.

One number in settings

A single integer stored in iOS user defaults. It marks where your free tables started counting from, so an existing user is not locked out by an update.

That is the complete list. What those files contain is roulette spin numbers, timestamps, internal identifiers and table settings — no name, no email address, no location, no contacts, no device identifiers, nothing personal about you. Vector never asks you for any of that, so it does not have it to lose.

Your data is yours, and only yours

Those files live on your device and are included in your normal iPhone backup, so they travel with you to a new phone. Nobody else can see them, including me. They are never transmitted anywhere, sold, shared, or used for advertising, and there is no analytics code watching what you tap.

Deleting the app deletes the lot. That also means I cannot recover it for you afterwards — I never had a copy. It is the honest trade-off that comes with not collecting anything.

Purchases

If you buy a subscription or the lifetime unlock, the purchase goes through Apple's StoreKit. Apple takes the payment and handles the billing, and your payment details go to Apple, never to me — I never see your card number, your name or your address.

All Vector asks the system is whether this Apple Account currently has a valid purchase. It gets back yes or no, and that answer stays on the device. Apple's own privacy policy covers their side of the transaction.

Children, and changes to this policy

Vector is for adults — 18 and over. It is not directed at children and it knowingly collects nothing from anyone, of any age.

If this policy ever changes, the updated version goes here with a new date at the top. If Vector ever started collecting anything, this page would say so before it shipped.
